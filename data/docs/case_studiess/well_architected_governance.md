# Case Study: Enterprise Well-Architected Governance Programme

## Organisation

Financial Services Enterprise

## Domain

Cloud Governance

## Challenge

The organisation operated hundreds of AWS accounts and needed a practical mechanism to assess architectural risk and prioritise remediation activity.

The challenge was not identifying risks.

The challenge was deciding which risks should actually be fixed.

## Role

Lead Cloud Architect

Responsible for:

* Well-Architected assessments
* Risk analysis
* Remediation planning
* Governance reporting
* Stakeholder engagement

## Scope

More than 300 AWS accounts.

## Approach

### Assessment

Conducted structured reviews across:

* Security
* Reliability
* Cost Optimisation
* Operational Excellence
* Performance Efficiency

### Triage Model

Each finding was classified as:

#### Remediate

Business value justified the effort.

#### Accept

Risk was understood and consciously accepted.

#### Investigate

Further analysis required before action.

### Governance Framework

Created reporting and prioritisation mechanisms to ensure effort focused on the highest-risk workloads.

## Key Architectural Decisions

### Risk-Based Prioritisation

Not all findings carry equal business impact.

Prioritisation should consider:

* Business criticality
* Regulatory exposure
* Operational impact
* Cost

### Governance over Compliance

The objective was improved outcomes rather than checklist completion.

## Results

* Improved visibility of enterprise cloud risks
* Reduced remediation backlog
* Increased stakeholder engagement
* Better alignment between business and technology teams

## Lessons Learned

Architectural governance is fundamentally a prioritisation exercise.

## Decision Pattern

Every risk does not require remediation.

Some risks should be accepted.
Some should be remediated.
Some should be investigated further.
